The stages of anaerobic digestion are: 

1. **Hydrolysis:** Complex polymers are broken down into
soluble monomers by extracellular enzymes.
2. **Acidogenesis:**  Acidogenic bacteria convert monomers into volatile fatty acids (VFA) like acetic, propionic, and butyric acids, along with CO2, H2, and ammonia.
3. **Acetogenesis:** Acetogenic bacteria convert VFAs into acetic acid (CH3COOH), hydrogen gas (H2), and carbon dioxide (CO2).
4. **Methanogenesis:** Strictly anaerobic methanogenic archaea reduce acetic acid or H2/CO2 to generate methane (CH4).

## 2. Describe the Biomass Gasification process in detail. Explain the four sequential stages inside the reactor and classify the different types of gasifiers.

### Biomass Gasification

Biomass gasification is a thermochemical process in which biomass is converted into a combustible gas called producer gas by heating it at high temperatures with a limited supply of air, oxygen or steam.

The producer gas mainly contains carbon monoxide (CO), hydrogen (H2), methane (CH4), carbon dioxide (CO2) and nitrogen (N2).

### Four Sequential Stages of Gasification

1. **Drying:** Moisture present in the biomass is removed by heating. This generally occurs at temperatures around 100–200°C.

2. **Pyrolysis:** Biomass is heated in the absence of oxygen and decomposes into solid charcoal, liquid tar and gases. It generally occurs around 200–600°C.

3. **Oxidation (Combustion):** A limited amount of oxygen reacts with charcoal and gases, producing heat. This heat supports the other gasification reactions. Temperatures may reach 800–1200°C.

4. **Reduction:** Hot charcoal reacts with carbon dioxide and steam to produce combustible gases like carbon monoxide and hydrogen. This generally occurs around 700–1000°C.

### Types of Gasifiers

1. **Updraft Gasifier:** Air enters from the bottom, while biomass moves downward. Producer gas exits from the top. It has high thermal efficiency but produces more tar.

2. **Downdraft Gasifier:** Both biomass and gas move downward. Air enters through the middle, and producer gas exits from the bottom. It produces relatively low-tar gas.

3. **Cross-draft Gasifier:** Air enters from one side and producer gas exits from the opposite side. It is suitable for small-scale applications and responds quickly to load changes.

4. **Fluidized Bed Gasifier:** Biomass is gasified in a bed of suspended particles using an upward flow of air or steam. It provides good mixing and uniform temperature.

## 3. Compare Horizontal Axis Wind Turbines (HAWT) and Vertical Axis Wind Turbines (VAWT), and explain the main components of a HAWT with a neat diagram.

### Difference Between HAWT and VAWT

| HAWT | VAWT |
|---|---|
| The axis of rotation is horizontal. | The axis of rotation is vertical. |
| Requires the turbine to face the wind. | Can receive wind from any direction. |
| Generally has higher efficiency. | Generally has lower efficiency. |
| Generator is located at the top of the tower. | Generator can be located near the ground. |
| Usually requires a yaw mechanism. | Does not require a yaw mechanism. |
| Installation and maintenance are comparatively difficult. | Maintenance can be easier. |
| Commonly used for large-scale power generation. | Commonly used for small-scale applications. |
| Examples: Propeller-type turbines. | Examples: Darrieus and Savonius turbines. |

### Main Components of HAWT

1. **Blades:** Capture wind energy and convert it into rotational mechanical energy.

2. **Rotor:** Consists of blades and a hub. It rotates when wind strikes the blades.

3. **Hub:** Connects the blades to the main shaft.

4. **Gearbox:** Increases the rotational speed of the rotor to drive the generator.

5. **Generator:** Converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.

6. **Nacelle:** Houses the gearbox, generator, shafts and other mechanical components.

7. **Tower:** Supports the turbine at a suitable height where wind speed is higher.

## 4. What is Geothermal Energy? Explain the different types of Geothermal Power Plants with their working mechanisms, advantages, and disadvantages.

### Geothermal Energy

Geothermal energy is the heat energy stored inside the Earth. It is obtained from hot rocks, underground water and steam present beneath the Earth's surface. This energy is used for electricity generation and heating.

### Types of Geothermal Power Plants

#### 1. Dry Steam Power Plant

**Working:**
- Steam from underground geothermal reservoirs is directly supplied to a turbine.
- The turbine drives a generator to produce electricity.
- The exhaust steam is condensed into water.
- The water is reinjected into the underground reservoir.

**Advantages:**
- Simple construction and operation.
- High efficiency.
- Requires less equipment.

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires naturally occurring underground steam reservoirs.
- Suitable locations are limited.
- Steam may contain corrosive gases and minerals.

**[Diagram Placeholder: Dry steam geothermal power plant showing underground steam reservoir, turbine, generator, condenser and reinjection well.]**

#### 2. Flash Steam Power Plant

**Working:**
- High-pressure hot water is extracted from underground reservoirs.
- The pressure is reduced in a flash tank, converting some hot water into steam.
- The steam drives a turbine connected to a generator.
- Exhaust steam is condensed, and water is reinjected underground.

**Advantages:**
- Suitable for high-temperature geothermal resources.
- Produces electricity efficiently.
- Can use naturally available hot water.

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires high-temperature water.
- Mineral deposits may damage equipment.
- Requires regular maintenance.

**[Diagram Placeholder: Flash steam geothermal power plant showing production well, flash tank, turbine, generator, condenser and injection well.]**

#### 3. Binary Cycle Power Plant

**Working:**
- Hot geothermal water passes through a heat exchanger.
- Heat is transferred to a secondary fluid having a low boiling point.
- The secondary fluid vaporizes and drives a turbine.
- The turbine operates a generator to produce electricity.
- The secondary fluid is condensed and reused, while geothermal water is reinjected.

**Advantages:**
- Can use comparatively low-temperature geothermal resources.
- Low emissions.
- Secondary fluid operates in a closed cycle.

**Disadvantages:**
- High initial installation cost.
- Requires heat exchangers and additional equipment.
- Lower efficiency at low resource temperatures.

## 5. Explain the working principle of a Hydrogen-Oxygen Fuel Cell with a neat sketch. Describe the different types of fuel cells and list their advantages and disadvantages.

### Hydrogen-Oxygen Fuel Cell

A hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell is an electrochemical device that converts the chemical energy of hydrogen and oxygen directly into electrical energy without combustion.

The overall chemical reaction is:

**2H2 + O2 → 2H2O + Electrical Energy + Heat**

### Working Principle

1. Hydrogen gas is supplied to the anode, where it splits into hydrogen ions and electrons.
2. The electrons travel through an external circuit, producing electric current.
3. Hydrogen ions pass through the electrolyte towards the cathode.
4. Oxygen is supplied to the cathode, where it combines with hydrogen ions and electrons to form water.
5. The process continues as long as hydrogen and oxygen are supplied.

For an acidic electrolyte:

**At Anode:**

2H2 → 4H+ + 4e-

**At Cathode:**

O2 + 4H+ + 4e- → 2H2O

### Different Types of Fuel Cells

1. **Alkaline Fuel Cell (AFC):** Uses an alkaline electrolyte, generally potassium hydroxide (KOH). It operates at relatively low temperatures.

2. **Proton Exchange Membrane Fuel Cell (PEMFC):** Uses a polymer membrane as an electrolyte. It operates at low temperatures and is commonly used in vehicles.

3. **Phosphoric Acid Fuel Cell (PAFC):** Uses phosphoric acid as an electrolyte and is suitable for stationary power generation.

4. **Molten Carbonate Fuel Cell (MCFC):** Uses molten carbonate salts as an electrolyte and operates at high temperatures.

5. **Solid Oxide Fuel Cell (SOFC):** Uses a solid ceramic electrolyte and operates at high temperatures. It is suitable for large-scale electricity generation.

### Advantages of Fuel Cells

1. High energy conversion efficiency.
2. Environment-friendly operation with very low pollution when using pure hydrogen.
3. Silent operation due to fewer moving parts.
4. Continuous electricity generation as long as fuel is supplied.
5. Low maintenance requirements.
6. Can be used in vehicles, industries and power plants.

### Disadvantages of Fuel Cells

1. High initial installation cost.
2. Hydrogen production and storage are expensive.
3. Hydrogen is highly flammable and requires careful handling.
4. Limited hydrogen refuelling infrastructure.
5. Some fuel cells require expensive catalysts such as platinum.
6. High-temperature fuel cells require special materials and insulation.
