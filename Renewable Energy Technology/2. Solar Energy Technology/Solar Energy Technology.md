## Geometry of Earth-Sun Technology
The earth-sun relationship drives our seasons, day length and climate. The key factors are: 

1. **Earth's tilt:** earth's axis of rotation is slanted approximately 23.5 degrees relative to the orbital plane. 
2. **Orbit:** earth follows an elliptical path around the sun. While this causes in small variation in distance, it is not het main reason for the season. 
3. **Parallelism:** as earth orbits, its axis reamins fixed in space, always pointing towards polaris (North Star) this means at different points in the orbit, different hemispheres are tilted towards or aways from the sun. This geometry results in the four turning points of the year.
    - **Summer Solstice (around 21 June):** the northern hemisphere is tilted most directly towards the sun. The sun's rays strike the tropic of cancer ($23.5\degree N$) vertically. This is the longest day of the year in the north, and the start of summer. 
    - **Winter Solstice (around 21 December):** the northern hemisphere is tilted most directly aways from the sun. The sun's rays strike the tropic of capricorn ($23.5\degree S$) vertically. This is the shortest day of the year, and the start of winter. 
    - **Vernal Equinox (around 21 March) & Autumunal Equinox (around 21 September):** during an equinox, earth's tilt is relatively sideways to the sun. Neighter hemisphere is tilted towards or away from the sun. The sun's rays strike the equator vertically. Day and night are approximately equal in length all over the world. 

## Sun-Earth Radiation Spectrum
The radiation balance between she sun and earth is defined by two distinct regions of the electromagnetic spectrum: incoming solar shortwave radiation and outgoing terrestrial longwave thermal radiation. This spectral seperation occurs because the emission wavelength of a body is inversely proportional to its absolute surface temperature. 

### Solar Spectrum (Shortwave)
- **Ultraviolet (100-400 nm):** contains high photon energy. Stratosphere ozone absorbs high-energy UV-C and most UV-B, protecting the surface. 
- **Visible light (400-700 nm):** passes through the atmosphere with minimal absorption, serving as earth's main illumination and solar energy driver. 
- **Near-Infrared (700-3000 nm):** partially absorbed by atmospheric water vapor and clouds before reaching the surface. 

### Terrestrial Spectrum (Longwave)
- **Thermal Infrared (4-100 $\mu$m):** absorbed heavily by greenhouse gases which re-readiate thermal heat back towards the surface (the natural greenhouse effect). 
- **Atmospheric Window (8-14 $\mu$m):** a narrow spectral range where greenhouse gas absorption is minimal, allowing thermal energy to escape directly in space. 

## Solar vs Terrestrial Radiation Spectrum 
| Property | Solar Radiation | Terrestrial Radiation | 
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Emission source | Solar photosphere | Earth surface & atmosphere | 
| Spectrum region | Shortwave radiation | Longwave thermal radiation | 
| Peak wavelength | 0.5 $\mu$m (visible green-blue) | 10 $\mu$m (thermal infrared) | 
| Spectral Distribution | UV (8%), Visible (43%), Near-IR (49%) | Mid to far-infrared (4-100 $\mu$m) | 
| Primary atmospheric absorbers | Ozone, water vapor | Water vapor, carbon dioxide, methane | 

## Solar Constant 
- The average amount of solar energy received per unit area at the top of earth's atmosphere. 
- Value $\approx$ 1367 $W/m^2$
- Represents the intensity of solar radiation before atmospheric effects.

## Solar Irradiation 
- THe total energy from the sun received on a surface over a given time. 
- Measured in $J/m^2$ or $Wh/m^2$.
- It's the time integrated form of solar power. 

## Solar Insolation 
- The total energy from the sun received on a given surface area during a specific time. 
- Often expressed in $kWh/m^2$ per day. 
- Varies with location, season and weather. 

## Solar Radiation
- The electromagnetic energy emitted by the sun. 
- Includes visible light, infrared and ultraviolet. 
- Drives earth's climate and energy systems. 

## Depletion of Solar Radiation
- As sunlight passes through the atmosphere, it is reduced by, 
    - **Absorption:** molecules like ozone, water vapor and $CO_2$ absorb certain wavelengths. 
    - **Scattering:** dust, aerosols and water molecules deflect radiation causing diffused light. 

## Pyranometer 
- **Purpose:** measures solar radiation flux density from the hemisphere above a flat surface. 
- **Working Principle:** 
    - Most pyranometers user a thermopile sensor. Sunlight heats blackened surface, creating a temperature difference between hot and cold junctions of thermocouples. 
    - This generates a small voltage proportional to solar irradiance. 
- **Spectral range:** typically measures radiation between 300 nm and 2800 nm (covers most of the solar spectrum reaching earth).
- **Cosine Response:** the sensor is designed to respond proportional to the cosine of the angle of incoming sunlight ensuring accurate reading at different sun angles. 

## Pyrheliometer 
A pyrheloimeter measures Direct Normal Irradiance (DNI), i.e., solar radiation received by a surface perpendicular to sun's rays. 
### Working Principle 
1. Sunlight enters through a collimator turbe (narrow field of view, ~5 degrees). 
2. It strikes a thermopile sensor or absorbed, which converts heat into an electrical signal. 
3. The output is expressed in watts per meter square ($W/m^2$) 

- **Mounting:** it is usually placed on a solar tracker that continuously aligns the instrument with the sun's position throughout the day. 

- Applications:
    1. Meterological and climate observations 
    2. Solar energy resource assessment (photovoltaic and concentrating solar power systems).
    3. Material testing under solar radiation.
