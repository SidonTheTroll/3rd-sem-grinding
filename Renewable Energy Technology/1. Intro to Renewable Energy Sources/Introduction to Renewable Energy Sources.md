## Classification of Energy Sources 
1. Renewable (solar, wind, geothermal, tidal) and Non-renewable (coal, petroleum, natural gas)
2. Primary and secondary 
3. Conventional and non-conventional 
## Forms of Energy 
1. Kinetic energy and potential energy 
2. Thermal and chemical energy 
3. Radiant and nuclear energy 
## Energy Chain or Energy Routes 
In ecology, an energy chain commonly called the food chain is a straight single path of energy transfer where one organism eats another. 
Energy routes is also called food web or energy flow pathway describes the complex, interconnected network of multiple feeding relationships and paths in an ecosystem. 
In engineering, an energy chain is a physical, hollow track that guides and protects moving hoses and electrical cables on industrial machines. 
## Electric Energy from Conventional Sources 
### Thermal Plant (Rankine Cycle)
A thermal power plant generates electrical energy from a conventional, non-renewable source by burning fossil fuels like coal, natural gas or oil to heat the water. This creates high pressure steam that spins a turbine, driving a generator to produce electricity for the powergrid. Major operators like Tata Power rely on these plants for dependable base load power. 

- How the process works: 
	1.  Fuel combustion 
	2. Steam production 
	3. Mechanical rotation 
	4. Electricity generation 
	5. Condensation and recycling 
## Integrated Gasification Combined Cycle 
An IGCC is an advanced thermal power station that converts solid fuels like coal into synthetic gas. It cleans the gas before burning it in a gas turbine and uses waste heat to drive a steam turbine, achieving higher efficiency and lower emissions than standard coal plants. 
## Gas Turbine Plant 
A gas turbine plant is an internal combustion system that uses pressurized air and burning fuel to spin a tubine. It draws in atmospheric air, compress it, mixes it with natural gas or little bit fuel and ignites it to create high velocity hot expanding gases that drives an electrical generator. 

**Main Parts**: compressor, combustion chamber, turbine and generator

## Nuclear Power Plant 
- **Nuclear fission:** a large heavy atom like uranium-235 splits into smaller parts after a tiny particle called a neutron hits it. 
- **Nuclear fusion:** two light atoms like hydrogen are forced together to make one heavier atom like helium. 
## New Technology 
Fuel cells, bio fuels and hydrogen energy system form an interconnected suite of clean tech solutions designed to replace fossil fuels. They generate power through electrochemical reactions or organic matter conversion, yielding high energy density, reguced carbon footprint and scalable applications for transport industry and powergrids. 

- **Fuel cell:** it is a reuauable electrochemical cell that converts chemical energy directly into heat or electricity for power. 
- **Bio fuel:** it is the fuel produced from biomass that undergoes anaerobic reactions to produce methane for combustion.
- **Hydrogen energy:** it is the energy obtained from hydrogen that is used through fuel cell for combustion to produce clean energy. 

## Energy Parameter 
- **Energy Policy:** a set of rules and goals made by a government to guide power production, pricing and use. 
- **Energy planning:** the process of looking at future power needed and matching them with available fuel orgreen resources.
- **Energy conservation:** the act of using less power by turning off idle machines or changing daily habits. 
- **Energy management:** a system used by business to track and lower its total power use over time. 
- **Energy Audit:** an inspection of a building or factory to find where power is wasted and how to fix it. 
- **Energy efficiency:** getting more work downe using the exact same amount of power or less. 
- **Energy intensity:** the total amount of power needed to produce one unit of economic output or money. 
- **Energy GDP Elasticity:** a ratio that shows how fast power use grows when the total economic GDP grows.
