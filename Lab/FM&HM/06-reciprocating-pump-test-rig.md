## Introduction 
A pump is a device, which lifts water from a lower level to a higher level at the expense of mechanical energy. Pump can be broadly classified into two categories, positive displacement & rotodynamic or dynamic pressure pumps. In a positive displacement pump, a small quantity of liquid is taken inside the pump and is displaced and forced out of the pump under pressure. The liquid inside a positive displacement pump may be subjected either to a reciprocating motion (reciprocating pump) or to a rotary/circular motion (gear pump, screw pump, etc.) 

## Theory  
Reciprocating pump consists a piston having a reciprocatory motion inside a cylinder with the help of connecting rod and a crack rotated by an electric motor, IC engine or any other suitable means. The cylinder is connected to the sump by the suction pipe and to the reservoir by the delivery pipe. Valves are provided at suction and delivery side and are non-returnable so that to allow the fluid in one direction only.  
These pumps are applied where the fluid is required in a small quantity but at a high pressure. Reciprocating pumps are applied for vehicle washing, water supply for multistory buildings, industries, etc. 

## Description 
The apparatus consists of a double acting cylinder reciprocating pump is operated on closed circuit basis. An AC motor with three speeds is provided to regulate the rpm of the pump. Suction and delivery head can be varied by the valves provided and Pressure and Vacuum Gauges can measure it. Flow of water is measured by using measuring tank and stopwatch.

## Experimental Procedure
**Starting Procedure:**

1. Firstly, we clean the apparatus and make sure the tanks are free from dust.
2. Then we close the drain valves provided.
3. Next, we fill the sump tank 3/4th with clean water and check that no foreign particles are present.
4. After that, we open the flow control valve on the water discharge line and the control valve on the suction line.
5. Then we make sure all the ON/OFF switches on the panel are in the OFF position.
6. Next, we set the speed of the pump using the step cone pulley.
7. Now we switch ON the main power supply and then switch ON the pump.
8. Then we operate the flow control valve to regulate the flow of water discharged by the pump.
9. After that, we operate the control valve to regulate the suction of the pump.
10. Next, we record the discharge pressure from the pressure gauge on the discharge line.
11. Then we record the suction pressure from the vacuum gauge at the suction of the pump.
12. After that, we note down the time taken for 10 pulses using a stopwatch, to calculate the power consumption.
13. Then we note down the RPM.
14. Next, we measure the flow of water discharged by the pump using a stopwatch and the measuring tank.
15. After that, we repeat the same procedure for different pressure heads.
16. Finally, we repeat the same procedure for different RPMs using the step cone pulley.

**Closing Procedure:**
1. Firstly, when the experiment is over, we properly open the gate valve on the discharge line.
2. Then we switch OFF the pump first.
3. Finally, we switch OFF the power supply to the panel (MCB).
