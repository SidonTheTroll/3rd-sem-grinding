- **Experiment no.:** 04 
- **Title of the Experiment:** Young's Modulus
- **Object of the Experiment:** To verify the Hooke's law and hence to calculate the modulus of elasticity of the material of a wire. 
- **Apparatus required:** Searle's apparatus, screw gauge, or slide calliper, meter scale, weights

## Theory 
Hooke's law states that within the elastic limit, stress developed in a body is directly proportional to the strain produced.  
Hence if the values of stress and strain at different loads are plotted the graph obtained will be a straight line.  
Again, $\text{Stress (tensile)}/\text{Strain (tensile)} = E$, the modulus of elasticity and tensile stress 
$$
F_1 = \frac{\text{Tensile load}}{\text{cross sectional area}} \\ 
\implies \frac{PL}{A}\ kg/cm^2 \\ 
\text{Tensile strain, } e_1 = \text{Elongation} / \text{Original Length} \\ 
\implies \delta l/l
$$

## Procedure 
1. The experimental wire is made light (to remove any kick) by putting dead loads at the hooks. 
2. The diameter of the wire is measured by means of screw gauge (taking the average of three reading at different length) and the cross sectional area (A) is calculated. 
3. The length of the experimental wire is measured by means of meter scale. 
4. The spirit level is brought to horizontal and the initial reading $(S_1)$ of the vernier is noted. 
5. A known weight is put gently in the hooko at the end of the wire. 
6. The spirit level is brought to horizontal (care being taken to note the screw always in the same direction) another final reading of the vernier scale $(S_2)$ is noted. 
7. The elongation $(\delta l)$ is found out by deducing $S_1$ from $S_2$. 
8. Load is gently put on the hook gradually and elongation at the different loads is calculated out by taking the vernier reading. 
9. The values of tensile stress and tensile strain are calculated out at different loads and stress-strain diagram is drawn (by plotting strain on the x-axis and stress on the y-axis). 
10. The Young's moduluss or modulus of elasticity is then calculated out.
