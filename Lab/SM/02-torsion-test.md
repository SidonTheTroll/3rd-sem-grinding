- **Experiment no.:** 02
- **Title of the Experiment:** Torsion Test
- **Object of the Experiment:** To determine the maximum shear stress developed in a rod subjected to torsion and hence to find the modulus of rigidity of the rod.

## Theory
From the torsion equation,

$$
\frac{T}{I_P} = \frac{G\theta}{l} = \frac{f_s}{R}
$$

where,  
T = torque or twisting moment in kg-cm  
$I_P$ = polar moment of inertia in $cm^4$  
G (or C) = modulus of rigidity in $kg/cm^2$  
$\theta$ = angle of twist in radians  
l = length of the rod in cm  
$f_s$ = maximum shear stress developed in the rod in $kg/cm^2$  
R = radius of the rod in cm  

We have,
1. $T = W \times r$, where W = weight applied at the pulley and r = radius of the pulley
2. $I_P \text{ (or J)} = (\pi / 32) \times d^4$, where d = diameter of the rod
3. $T = (\pi / 16) \times f_s d^3$, so $f_s = 16T / (\pi d^3)$
4. $G = (T \times l) / (I_P \theta)$

## Procedure
1. Find the circumference $(2\pi r)$ of the pulley by wrapping a thread around it and measuring the thread with a meter scale. From this, calculate the radius (r).
2. Measure the diameter (d) of the rod with a screw gauge or vernier caliper (taking the average of three values at different lengths), and calculate the polar moment of inertia $(I_P)$.
3. Fix both pointers at the zero mark of the graduated scales, and measure the distance of each pointer from the fixed end of the rod with a meter scale.
4. Place a known weight (W) gently on the pan hanging over the pulley.
5. Note down the angle of twist ($\theta$) given by the pointers on the graduated scales (at both lengths of the rod).
6. Repeat the above procedure, noting the readings of both pointers, with different weights on the pan.
7. Calculate the values of shear stress and modulus of rigidity.
