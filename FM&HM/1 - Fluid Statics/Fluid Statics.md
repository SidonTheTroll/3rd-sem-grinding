Fluid is a substance that has the ability to flow or deform continuously under the action of external shear force. Generally, liquids, gas, and vapor fall in the category of fluids. E.g.: air, water, steam, mercury, etc. 

## Classification of Fluid 
A liquid may be classified as 

| Category | Types                   |
| -------- | ----------------------- |
| A        | Liquid, Gas, Vapor      |
| B        | Ideal fluid, Real fluid |

1. **Liquid:** a liquid is a fluid which possesses a definite volume (which varies only slightly with temperature and pressure). 
2. **Gas:** it has no definite volume and is compressible. 
3. **Vapor:** It is a gas whose temperature and pressure such that it is very near to the liquid state. E.g.: steam, water vapor. 
4. **Ideal Fluids:** an ideal fluid is one which has no viscosity and surface tension and is incompressible. In true sense, no such fluids exist in nature. 
5. **Real fluids:** a real fluid is one which has viscosity, surface tension, and compressible in addition to density. The real fluids are available in nature. 

## Properties of Fluids 
1. **Density ($\rho$):** it is defined as the ratio of mass per unit volume at standard temperature and pressure. 
$$ 
\rho = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}} \ \text{Unit:} kg/m^3
$$
2. **Weight Density (w):** it is defined as the ratio of weight per unit volume at standard temperature and pressure, i.e., 
$$
	 w = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}} = \frac{mg}{v} = \rho \cdot g
$$
	- In SI unit, unit of weight density is $KN/m^3$. 

3. **Specific volume (v):** it is defined as the ratio of volume per unit mass, i.e., 
$$
	 \text{Specific volume,}\ v = \frac{\text{volume}}{\text{mass}} = \frac{1}{\rho}
$$
4. **Specific Gravity:** it is defined as the ratio of specific weight of liquid to the specific weight of standard fluid. 
	- For liquids, standard fluid is water and for gas, it is air. 

5. **Compressibility and Bulk Modulus:** the property by virtue of which fluids undergo a change in volume under the action of external pressure is known as compressiblity. Elasticity of fluids is measured in terms of bulk modulus of elasticity (K) which is defined as the ratio of compressive stress to volumetric strain. Compressibility is the reciprocal of bulk modulus of elasticity.
