## 1. Explain the corrosion mechanism. What are the different methods of preventing corrosion.

**Corrosion** is the gradual destruction of a metal due to chemical or electrochemical reactions with its surroundings.

**Electrochemical mechanism of corrosion:**

1. When a metal is exposed to moisture and oxygen, tiny anodic and cathodic areas are formed on its surface.
2. At the anode, metal loses electrons and dissolves into the solution as metal ions.\
   \\[\
   Fe \rightarrow Fe^{2+}+2e^-\
   \\]
3. The released electrons move towards the cathode.
4. At the cathode, oxygen reacts with water and electrons to form hydroxide ions.
5. These ions react further to form rust (hydrated iron oxide) in the presence of oxygen and moisture.

The following methods are commonly used to prevent corrosion:

1. **Painting:** Applying paint on the metal surface prevents contact with air and moisture.
2. **Galvanization:** Coating iron or steel with a layer of zinc to prevent rusting.
3. **Electroplating:** Coating a metal with another metal such as chromium, nickel or tin.
4. **Oiling and Greasing:** Applying oil or grease to prevent direct contact with moisture and air.
5. **Cathodic Protection:** Making the metal act as a cathode by using a sacrificial anode such as zinc or magnesium.

## What is plastic? What are the raw materials for plastic? What are the different types of thermo-setting plastic?

Plastic is a synthetic or semi-synthetic material made from polymers. It can be easily moulded into different shapes by applying heat and pressure.

The main raw materials used for manufacturing plastics are:

1. Coal
2. Petroleum
3. Natural gas
4. Cellulose
5. Natural resins

Thermosetting plastics are plastics that become permanently hard when heated and cannot be remoulded.

The different types are:

1. **Bakelite:** Used in electrical switches, plugs and handles.
2. **Melamine:** Used in kitchen utensils and laminates.
3. **Epoxy Resin:** Used in adhesives, coatings and electrical insulation.
4. **Urea Formaldehyde:** Used in electrical fittings and adhesives.
5. **Polyester Resin:** Used in fibre-reinforced plastics and automobile parts.

## How do thermoplastics differ from thermosetting materials?

| Thermoplastics                                        | Thermosetting Plastics                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| 1. They soften on heating and harden on cooling.      | 1. They become permanently hard on heating.   |
| 2. They can be remoulded and reused.                  | 2. They cannot be remoulded or reused.        |
| 3. They have a linear or slightly branched structure. | 3. They have a highly cross-linked structure. |
| 4. They are generally recyclable.                     | 4. They are difficult to recycle.             |
| 5. Examples: PVC, Polythene and Nylon.                | 5. Examples: Bakelite, Melamine and Epoxy.    |

## Briefly explain the production process of plastics

The production of plastics involves the following steps:

1. **Raw Material Preparation:** Raw materials such as petroleum, natural gas and coal are processed to obtain monomers.
2. **Polymerization:** Monomers are chemically combined to form long-chain polymers.
3. **Addition of Additives:** Colouring agents, plasticizers, stabilizers and fillers are added to improve properties.
4. **Moulding:** The plastic material is heated and shaped into the required form using moulding processes.
5. **Finishing:** The final product is cooled, trimmed and finished for use.
