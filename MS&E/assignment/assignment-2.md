## 1. Explain the corrosion mechanism. What are the different methods of preventing corrosion.

**Corrosion** is the gradual destruction of a metal due to chemical or electrochemical reactions with its surroundings.

**Electrochemical mechanism of corrosion:**

1. When a metal is exposed to moisture and oxygen, tiny anodic and cathodic areas are formed on its surface.
2. At the anode, metal loses electrons and dissolves into the solution as metal ions.
   $$
   Fe \rightarrow Fe^{2+}+2e^-\
   $$
3. The released electrons move towards the cathode.
4. At the cathode, oxygen reacts with water and electrons to form hydroxide ions.
5. These ions react further to form rust (hydrated iron oxide) in the presence of oxygen and moisture.

The following methods are commonly used to prevent corrosion:

1. **Painting:** Applying paint on the metal surface prevents contact with air and moisture.
2. **Galvanization:** Coating iron or steel with a layer of zinc to prevent rusting.
3. **Electroplating:** Coating a metal with another metal such as chromium, nickel or tin.
4. **Oiling and Greasing:** Applying oil or grease to prevent direct contact with moisture and air.
5. **Cathodic Protection:** Making the metal act as a cathode by using a sacrificial anode such as zinc or magnesium.

## 2. What is plastic? What are the raw materials for plastic? What are the different types of thermo-setting plastic?

Plastic is a synthetic or semi-synthetic material made from polymers. It can be easily moulded into different shapes by applying heat and pressure.

The main raw materials used for manufacturing plastics are:

1. Coal
2. Petroleum
3. Natural gas
4. Cellulose
5. Natural resins

Thermosetting plastics are plastics that become permanently hard when heated and cannot be remoulded.

The different types are:

1. **Bakelite:** Used in electrical switches, plugs and handles.
2. **Melamine:** Used in kitchen utensils and laminates.
3. **Epoxy Resin:** Used in adhesives, coatings and electrical insulation.
4. **Urea Formaldehyde:** Used in electrical fittings and adhesives.
5. **Polyester Resin:** Used in fibre-reinforced plastics and automobile parts.

## 4. Briefly explain the production process of plastics

Plastics are produced by converting raw materials into long-chain polymers and then shaping them into useful products. The main steps are:

1. **Raw Material Preparation:** Crude oil, natural gas and coal are the main sources. These are refined and processed (for example, by cracking naphtha or ethane) to obtain simple molecules called monomers, such as ethylene, propylene and styrene.

2. **Polymerization:** Monomers are chemically joined to form long-chain polymers. This happens in two main ways: *addition polymerization* (e.g. polyethylene, PVC, polystyrene) and *condensation polymerization* (e.g. nylon, polyester). Heat, pressure and catalysts are used to control the reaction.

3. **Addition of Additives:** The raw polymer is mixed with additives to improve its properties. Coloring agents (pigments) give color, plasticizers add flexibility, stabilizers protect against heat and UV light, and fillers reduce cost and add strength.

4. **Pelletizing:** The compounded plastic is melted, extruded into strands and cut into small pellets or granules, which are easy to store and transport to manufacturers.

5. **Moulding:** The pellets are heated until soft and shaped using processes such as injection moulding, blow moulding, extrusion or compression moulding, depending on the product.

6. **Cooling and Finishing:** The shaped product is cooled to set its form, then trimmed, polished, printed or assembled before being packed for use.

## 5.What are raw materials for plastics? Write down the advantages of plastics. How many types of plastics are there? Mention them with example.

The main raw materials used for manufacturing plastics are:

1. Petroleum
2. Natural gas
3. Coal
4. Cellulose
5. Natural resins

Their advantages are:

1. They are lightweight and easy to handle.
2. They are resistant to corrosion and chemicals.
3. They are good electrical and thermal insulators.
4. They can be easily moulded into different shapes.
5. They are generally low-cost and durable.
6. They require less maintenance.

Plastics are mainly classified into **two types**:

**1. Thermoplastics:** These plastics soften on heating and harden on cooling. They can be remoulded and reused.

Examples: PVC, Polythene, Nylon and Acrylic.

**2. Thermosetting Plastics:** These plastics become permanently hard when heated and cannot be remoulded.

Examples: Bakelite, Melamine, Epoxy and Urea Formaldehyde.
